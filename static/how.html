How Baseline works

Most watchlists tell you that a stock moved. Baseline tries to tell you when that movement is actually unusual.

1
Add a stock Choose the stocks you want Baseline to watch.
2
Build its baseline Baseline learns how much that particular stock normally moves.
3
Compare today's move Today's movement is compared with the stock's own recent behaviour.
4
See what changed Unusual moves are highlighted so you know what deserves a closer look.

Why not just use a fixed 5% alert?

A fixed threshold treats every stock the same. But stocks behave very differently.

A stock that normally moves 0.3% a day suddenly moves 4%. That's unusual.

A stock that normally moves 5% suddenly moves 5%. That's normal for that stock.

A fixed alert misses the first situation or creates too much noise in the second. Baseline adapts to the stock instead.

The question isn't “Did the stock move a lot?”
It's “Did the stock move a lot compared with what is normal for it?”

Volume adds context

Price movement doesn't tell the whole story. Baseline also looks at trading volume to add context to an unusual move.

3.6× normal volume — the move has much broader trading activity.

0.3× normal volume — the move happened on unusually thin trading.

The second case is deliberately treated with caution. Price alone isn't enough to claim that something significant happened.

What happens when nothing happens?

Nothing unusual is a useful result. If none of your stocks crossed their normal range, Baseline stays quiet. The goal isn't to manufacture alerts — it's to surface meaningful changes.

New stocks need time

A new stock needs roughly 20 days of history before Baseline has enough information to make a meaningful comparison. Until then, it says it doesn't know rather than pretending it does.

Data and limitations

Prices come from Yahoo Finance and may be delayed or temporarily unavailable. Baseline shows the age of the data and whether the market is open, so a closing price isn't mistaken for a live price.

Baseline tells you what changed. It does not predict what happens next and it isn't investment advice. An unusual move is a reason to look, not a reason to act.
