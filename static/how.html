How Baseline works

Why this app says what it says.

The problem with "alert me at 5%"

Most watchlists flag a stock when it moves more than some fixed amount. That treats every stock the same, and stocks aren't the same.

A stock that normally drifts 0.3% a day moves 4%. Something happened.

A stock that routinely swings 5% moves 5%. That's a Tuesday.

A fixed threshold misses the first and cries wolf on the second. Set it low and everything is flagged, which means nothing is. Set it high and you miss real events on calmer stocks.

What Baseline does instead

Every stock is judged against its own recent behaviour. Baseline looks at the last 30 trading days, works out how much that particular stock typically moves in a day, and asks how far today sits from that.

A move roughly two standard deviations from normal gets flagged — about the most extreme 5% of days for that stock. Everything else stays quiet.

Volume changes the story

A big price move on heavy trading means a lot of people acted. The same move on thin trading might be one large order. Baseline says which:

On 3.6× normal volume — this looks like a real event.

On 0.3× normal volume — this could be a single large trade rather than broad activity.

The second reading is deliberately hedged. Price alone isn't enough to claim something happened.

When it says nothing

If no stock crossed the line, Baseline says so plainly. "Nothing unusual" is a real answer, not a failure to find something. An app that always has something urgent to report is an app you stop trusting.

What it won't claim

New stocks need about 20 days of history before there's anything to compare against. Until then Baseline says it doesn't know, rather than guessing.

Prices come from Yahoo Finance and can be delayed or briefly unavailable. Every stock shows how old its data is, and whether the market is open — a price you see at 9pm is a closing price, not a live one.

What this isn't

Baseline tells you what changed. It doesn't tell you what will happen next, and it isn't advice. An unusual move is a reason to look, not a reason to act.
